\section*{Comparing Measurements: T-Score}
\begin{center}
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{|\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2|}{\sqrt{\delta\bar{x_1}^2 + \delta\bar{x_2}^2}}
\end{equation}
\end{center}
Intuitively this measures the difference in the mean in units of the uncertainity. Larger/smaller values of $t$ mean a larger/smaller discrepancy between the values.

We will use a convention where:
\begin{center}
$t < 1$ is considered \textit{indistinguishable}\\
$1 < t < 3$ is considered \textit{inconclusive}\\
$3 < t$ is considered \textit{distingushable}\\
\end{center}
\textbf{NOTE:} the language is "indistigusibale" not "the same". In science we falsify or fail to falsify modelsm we don't "confirm" them.

\section*{Propagation of Uncertainty}
\begin{itemize}
\item Each uncertainty contributes to the total uncertainty proportional to the partial derivative (slope of $f$ with respect to that variable).
\item Multiple sources don't simply add but add "in quadrature", since they are as likely to cancel as add.
\item We will always give you the needed derivatives.
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{equation}
\delta f = \sqrt{\sum_{i}(\frac{\delta f}{\delta x_i} \delta x_i)^2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta f(x,y) = \sqrt{(\frac{\delta f}{\delta x} \delta x)^2 + (\frac{\delta f}{\delta y} \delta y)^2}
\end{equation}
\end{center}
There are three steps to getting the propagation of uncertainty formula: \textbf{Terms, Variables, \& Derivatives}.
\begin{center}
\begin{equation}
\delta f(z_1, z_2, ..., z_N) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N} (\frac{\delta f}{\delta z_i} \delta z_i)^2}
\end{equation}
\end{center}
\subsection*{Terms}
Start with the general propogation of uncertainty formula and \textit{write out the same number of terms as you have uncertainties} (three in this example: $\delta m, \delta v, \delta r$)
\begin{center}
\begin{equation}
\delta f(z_1, z_2, z_3) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\frac{\delta f}{\delta z_i} \delta z_i)^2} \equiv \sqrt{(\frac{\delta f}{\delta z_1} \delta z_1)^2 + (\frac{\delta f}{\delta z_2} \delta z_2)^2 + (\frac{\delta f}{\delta z_3} \delta z_3)^2}
\end{equation}
\end{center}
\subsection*{Variables}
Associate each variable with a $z_i$ (order doesn't matter) and associate the function with $f$
\begin{center}
\begin{align*}
z_1 \mapsto m \\
z_2 \mapsto v \\
z_3 \mapsto r \\
f \mapsto F_c
\end{align*}
\end{center}
Then directly substitute into the formula from the \textit{Terms} step.
\begin{center}
\begin{equation}
\delta F_c (m, v, r) = \sqrt{(\frac{\delta F_c}{\delta m} \delta m)^2 + (\frac{\delta F_c}{\delta v} \delta v)^2 + (\frac{\delta F_c}{\delta r} \delta r)^2}
\end{equation}
\end{center}
\subsection*{Derivatives}
Substitute in the derivatives from equation (1) to get the \textbf{correct formula}
\begin{center}
 \[
 \boxed{\sqrt{(\frac{v^2}{r} \delta m)^2 + (\frac{2 mv}{r} \delta v)^2 + (-\frac{m v^2}{r^2} \delta r)^2}}
 \]
\end{center}
